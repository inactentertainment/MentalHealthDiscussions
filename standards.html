Trust & Standards

Useful information needs visible boundaries.

MHD is designed to make complicated mental-health information easier to navigate without pretending that a website can replace a clinician, attorney, crisis professional, peer-support relationship, or local community organization. These standards explain how the project should earn trust as it grows.

What MHD is

A navigation and education layer.

The strongest MHD pages help visitors understand a question, prepare useful information, identify the right system or professional, and move to the next step.

Education

Translate complexity

Explain conditions, treatments, medications, rights, systems, and research in plain language while preserving uncertainty and important limitations.

Preparation

Organize before the conversation

Help visitors prepare timelines, medication lists, observations, questions, goals, records, and decision points before appointments or system interactions.

Navigation

Connect the doors

Make it easier to move among families, providers, hospitals, insurers, work/school systems, state rules, crisis services, public agencies, and community resources.

Evidence labels

Not every source proves the same thing.

MHD should tell visitors what kind of information they are looking at rather than flattening research, policy, professional guidance, and lived experience into one category.

Established / standardBroadly accepted clinical practice or strong support for a specific use.
Condition-specificEvidence applies to particular diagnoses, populations, or treatment contexts.
Mixed / emergingResearch is limited, inconsistent, indirect, or still developing.
System / policyLaw, regulation, budgets, administrative data, service research, access, or implementation.
Lived experiencePersonal or family experience that can illuminate impact but does not establish effectiveness.
Source hierarchy

Start as close to the source as practical.

The best source depends on the question. MHD should prefer authoritative primary or high-quality synthesis sources and clearly identify when a page uses interpretation or secondary reporting.

Clinical / scientific

Research and professional guidance

  • Government health agencies and official safety communications
  • Peer-reviewed research and systematic reviews
  • Professional guidelines and consensus documents when relevant
  • Direct links to supporting material where practical
Law / policy

Official legal and government sources

  • Statutes, regulations, court or agency materials
  • Official state/federal websites
  • Budgets, program rules, dockets, notices, and public reports
  • Secondary explanations labeled as explanations—not law itself
Experience

Stories with the right label

  • Lived experience can show consequences and practical realities
  • Stories do not prove that a treatment works
  • Personal accounts should protect privacy and avoid false universality
  • Opinion should remain distinguishable from evidence
Content lifecycle

Publish. Review. Correct. Improve.

A trustworthy resource needs a visible process after publication—not just good intentions at launch.

01

Define the question

State what the page is trying to answer and what it is not designed to answer.

02

Use traceable sources

Prefer authoritative sources and preserve links so claims can be rechecked later.

03

Label limits

Make uncertainty, scope, evidence level, jurisdiction, and safety boundaries visible.

04

Review and correct

Show review dates where feasible and provide a route to report factual, source, accessibility, or wording concerns.

Safety boundaries

MHD should be helpful without crossing into roles it does not hold.

These lines are part of the product—not fine print that disappears when a tool becomes convenient.

No diagnosisMHD can explain conditions and symptom patterns but does not determine what diagnosis a visitor has.
No prescribingMHD can explain medication information and questions to ask but does not choose a drug, dose, taper, or treatment plan.
No emergency assessmentImmediate danger, overdose, severe medical instability, or inability to stay safe should move to live crisis or emergency support.
No individualized legal adviceMHD can explain laws, rights, procedures, and public systems but does not represent a person or give case-specific legal advice.
No invented partnership claimsMHD should not imply endorsement, affiliation, clinical review, or joint programming without an actual agreement.
No false certaintyWhen evidence is mixed, a law is jurisdiction-specific, or a system varies locally, the page should say so.

Partnership standards

Complement strong organizations instead of copying them.

MHD partnerships should make navigation easier while preserving the partner’s own professional, clinical, peer-support, community, or institutional role.

Clear roles

Know who is responsible for what

MHD provides educational navigation. Partners retain responsibility for their clinical care, peer support, crisis response, employment/school decisions, legal services, or organizational programs.

Transparent status

Say whether something is live, pilot-ready, or future

Program status should be explicit so ambition is not mistaken for current capacity.

Data restraint

Collect less, protect more

Future analytics, surveys, or systems research should collect only what is necessary, minimize sensitive data, and establish appropriate privacy and governance before publication.
