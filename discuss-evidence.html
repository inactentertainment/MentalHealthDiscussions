MHD Evidence, Experience & Discussion

What do we know, what do people experience, and what still needs better answers?

Articles, research explainers, YouTube, future interviews, lived experience, and public discussion - clearly labeled so evidence, opinion, personal experience, and emerging research do not blur into one another.

MHD trust rule: a personal story can be important without proving a treatment works, and a study can be useful without answering every real-life question. This hub labels the kind of information being presented and the strength of the evidence behind it.
Established / standardStrongly supported for a specific use or broadly accepted clinical practice.
Condition-specific evidenceGood evidence exists, but only for particular diagnoses, populations, or treatment situations.
Mixed / uncertainResearch is inconsistent, limited, indirect, or still evolving.
System / policy evidenceAdministrative data, law, budgets, service research, access data, or implementation evidence.
Lived experiencePersonal or family experience that can illuminate impact but does not establish clinical effectiveness.

Interview & Lived-Experience Standard

Real voices without turning stories into proof.

The interview library is now structurally ready even before a large catalog of interviews exists.

Interview label

Every interview should identify the speaker's role: lived experience, family/caregiver, clinician, researcher, advocate, public official, system leader, or other relevant role.

Evidence boundary

Personal experience should be labeled as experience. Clinical claims made during interviews should be linked to supporting evidence or clearly identified as opinion, uncertainty, or an area needing verification.

Privacy + dignity

Do not require unnecessary diagnostic details, trauma details, medication history, legal history, or family information to make someone's story meaningful.

Corrections + context

If an interview contains an important factual error, MHD can preserve the conversation while adding a visible correction or context note rather than silently rewriting what was said.

MHD Editorial Trust Layer

What every major content item should tell you.

This is now the standard used by the hub and the shared trust strip used across major MHD pages.

Source type

Clinical guidance, regulatory source, statute, public-health data, peer-reviewed research, administrative data, or lived experience.

Evidence level

Established, condition-specific, mixed/uncertain, emerging, system/policy, safety caution, or lived experience.

Last reviewed

A visible date tells visitors when MHD last checked the page or content item against its source base.

Corrections

Visitors should have a direct route to report a source, wording, accessibility, legal, or factual concern for review.

Related tools

Evidence explainers should connect to the useful next action: medication, treatment, family, appointment prep, find care, crisis, law, or advocacy.

No false balance

Popularity and controversy do not automatically deserve equal weight with strong evidence. Uncertainty should be described rather than hidden.

Medical boundaries

Educational summaries never become personalized diagnosis, prescribing, or emergency assessment.

Policy boundaries

MHD can explain policy and advocacy mechanics without selecting a partisan position or presenting advocacy as individualized legal advice.
