Tool 09

Complementary & emerging approaches

This is the second lane you asked for. “People are using it” and “research proves it works” are not the same statement, so MHD shows both interest and evidence level.

Important: Complementary approaches should not be used to delay urgent evaluation or replace effective care for severe symptoms. Herbs and supplements can interact with psychiatric and other medications.
How MHD reads evidence

No false balance.

We will cover conventional care, non-drug care, complementary approaches and emerging treatments—but we will not make weak evidence look equal to strong evidence.

Established / standard

Widely supported

Evidence-based treatments or broadly accepted clinical practices with substantial support for a specific condition.

Some evidence

Potentially useful

Research suggests benefit, often modest or condition-specific, but limitations remain.

Uncertain / mixed

Still unclear

Studies disagree, are too small, or do not yet show clinically meaningful benefit.

Safety caution

Risk matters

An approach may have interactions, known harms, or special risks for particular diagnoses.

Emerging research

Worth watching

Actively studied but not necessarily FDA-approved or established as routine treatment.

Lived experience

Useful, but different

People’s experiences can identify questions and practical issues, but they do not prove effectiveness.
