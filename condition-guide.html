How Evaluation Works

A good assessment looks beyond the label.

Mental-health diagnosis is usually based on pattern, time course, severity, impairment, history, and exclusion of other plausible causes—not one symptom or online checklist.

1. What changed?Symptoms, onset, duration, baseline, triggers, sleep, functioning.
2. Lifetime patternPast episodes, childhood history, trauma, prior treatment, family history.
3. What else could explain it?Medical illness, medications, substances, sleep, neurological conditions, overlapping disorders.
4. What helps next?Working diagnosis, treatment options, monitoring, follow-up, and revision if the picture changes.
Urgent Assessment

Some symptoms should not wait for routine care.

These are examples, not a complete emergency checklist.

Immediate safety risk

Suicidal intent, violent intent, overdose, inability to stay safe, or a dangerous level of impulsivity requires live crisis/emergency assessment.

New severe confusion or psychosis

First or rapidly worsening psychosis, delirium-like confusion, severe disorganization, catatonia-like symptoms, or sudden neurological change needs prompt professional evaluation.

Medical instability

Fainting, chest pain, severe dehydration, breathing problems, major medication reactions, intoxication/withdrawal, or eating-disorder medical warning signs can be medical emergencies.
